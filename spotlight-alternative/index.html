What Spotlight does well

It’s already on your Mac, it searches everything macOS indexes, mail and messages included, and since macOS 26 it runs actions from apps and Shortcuts and keeps a short clipboard history. If that covers your day, you don’t need another launcher.

What Copperlight adds

  • Windows and menus from the keyboard. :left, :top left, :nudge left and :restore, saved layouts (:layout work), and any menu item of the app you’re in: :export pdf.
  • Whatever you selected, already typed when it opens, and ↑ for what you ran before.
  • Your Mac’s numbers: battery, disk, ip, and port 3000 to see and end what’s listening.
  • A longer clipboard history of text and images, on macOS 15 too, that skips your password manager.
  • Chips. Press Tab on a tool’s name and what you type goes straight to it: translate, stocks, timers.
  • The little tools: snippets, notes in Markdown, a pomodoro, timers, today’s meetings, emoji, JSON and UUIDs.
  • Your own commands in one plain-text file, and any script you drop in a folder.
  • Nothing to sign in to, no telemetry, and source code you can read.

Switching from Spotlight

Copperlight opens with ⌥Space, so it can sit next to Spotlight. To give it ⌘Space instead:

  1. In System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts… → Spotlight, turn off Show Spotlight search.
  2. Open Copperlight’s Settings, click the shortcut and press ⌘Space.

Files are found through the index Spotlight already keeps, in the folders you choose, so there’s nothing new to index.

Other Spotlight alternatives

Raycast and Alfred are the best-known launchers for Mac, and both are good at what they do. Here’s how Copperlight compares with each.

Copperlight vs RaycastRaycast has an extension store, AI, and apps for Windows and iPhone. Copperlight has no account, no AI service, and open source code. Copperlight vs AlfredAlfred has deep workflows and runs on older Macs. Copperlight has clipboard history, snippets and window commands built in.

Questions

What’s the best free Spotlight alternative for Mac?

It depends on what you need. Copperlight is free and open source, with clipboard history, window commands and snippets built in. Raycast is known for its extension store and AI, and Alfred for its workflows.

Does Spotlight have clipboard history?

Since macOS 26, yes: it keeps what you copy for 8 hours, and from macOS 26.1 you can choose 30 minutes, 8 hours or 7 days. Copperlight keeps text and images for 90 days by default, on macOS 15 and later.

Can Copperlight use the ⌘Space shortcut?

Yes. Turn off Spotlight’s shortcut in System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts… → Spotlight, then set ⌘Space in Copperlight’s Settings.

Does Copperlight use Spotlight’s index?

For files, yes. It searches the index macOS already keeps, in the folders you pick, so there’s nothing extra to index.

Can I keep both Spotlight and Copperlight?

Yes. Copperlight opens with ⌥Space, so both work side by side.
